    The idea behind Button Counter

    Button Counter began as a personal project. I didn’t only want to know where I stood, but also what I could do to get where I wanted to be.

    So I started calculating. I laid out my own situation and changed one thing at a time.

    What if I put in more?What if I keep working for longer?What if I invest differently?

    After every change, I saw what it did. That showed me which strategy suits me best, not only to work out where I wanted to end up, but also to get there.

    I want to give you that same insight.

    So my own calculations became an app for anyone with the same question. Whether you’re just starting out, in the middle of building up, or retirement is already in sight: you enter your own situation and try out what makes the difference.

    And because it’s your money, everything stays in your own browser.

    Because it isn’t only about what you build up, but above all about what you can do with it.

    Frequently asked questions

    What is Button Counter?

    Button Counter gives you a view of your financial future: how long your savings will last and what changes when you adjust something. You enter your age, your savings and what you want to spend each month. Button Counter then shows the chance that your money lasts until your end age, based on 4,000 simulations of good and bad market years.

    Why Button Counter instead of a simple calculator?

    A simple calculator answers one sum, such as how long an amount lasts or how much tax you pay on savings. Button Counter puts everything in one plan: your savings, your pension and state pension, work per period, one or two people and the rules of your country. Instead of one number you see the chance that your money is enough, based on 4,000 simulations. Every number shows whether it comes from a source, is an assumption or was entered by you.

    How much savings do I need to stop working?

    That depends on your monthly budget, your age and the pension you will receive. Button Counter calculates the amount you need for a 90% chance that your money is enough. As a rough rule of thumb, people often need 25 to 30 times the yearly spending that their pensions do not cover.

    How is the chance of success calculated?

    Button Counter runs 4,000 simulations in which returns go up and down each year. It does this for four mixes (defensive, balanced, aggressive, and savings without investing), after costs, tax and inflation, so every amount is in today’s money. The chance of success is the share of simulations in which your money lasts until your end age. Two crash scenarios, one like 2008 and one like 2022, are shown separately.

    Does my spending stay the same in the calculation, or does it fall?

    You choose this under “How does your spending develop later?”. With Flat, your spending stays the same every year, in today’s money. That is the most cautious choice, and it is free. Research in several countries shows that people spend differently in different stages of life. With Pro you can plan with such a pattern. Under each option you see how much you need, so you can see what the choice makes of a difference. These are averages: your own spending may develop differently.

    Which countries does Button Counter support?

    Button Counter has its own rules for the Netherlands, Belgium, Germany, France, Spain, Italy, Portugal, Ireland, the United Kingdom and the United States, plus a general setting for other countries. Each country has its own state pension, pension age and tax on investments. Button Counter also compares what your budget is worth in 31 European countries, based on Eurostat price levels.

    Is Button Counter free, and what happens to my data?

    Button Counter has no advertising and needs no account. Most of it is free and stays free; a few extended features are closed off on purpose as Pro. What you enter stays in your own browser on your own device and is not sent to Button Counter. Visits and the use of features are counted anonymously, without cookies.

    Is this financial advice?

    No. Button Counter is a general calculation tool that gives estimates based on simplified rules and assumptions. It does not give advice about your personal situation. Check official sources and consult an independent, licensed adviser before you make important financial decisions.

    Privacy

    Your data stays with you. What you enter (ages, amounts, choices) is stored only in your own browser on this device. It is not sent to Button Counter and nobody else can see it. If you clear your browser data, it is gone.

    No tracking. No advertising cookies are used and you are not followed. Visits are counted anonymously (the number of visits, pages and country, and how often features such as the notepad or sharing are used, never what you enter) with GoatCounter, without cookies and without personal data. The storage in your browser only makes the site work and remembers your input.

    What does run through others. The site is hosted on GitHub Pages, so GitHub sees technical data such as your IP address when the page loads. The form behind “Share a tip” is a Google Form: what you write there reaches the maker and possibly Google. Don’t enter anything there that you want to keep private. Links to sources lead to other websites with their own rules.

    Contact. What you send through “Share a tip” is used only to improve the site and is not kept longer than needed. Want something deleted, or have a question about this text? Send a message through “Share a tip”.
